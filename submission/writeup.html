The Relay

What I built and why

I built a Python pipeline on Groq's free tier. A router (qwen3.8-27b) picks the paper and rewrites the question, a search tool (BM25) fetches 3 pieces of 2400 characters, a decide step refuses weak evidence, gpt-oss-120b drafts with a citation on every sentence, and a check verifies each citation, number and quote before release, rewriting a failed draft once.

The decision that shaped it most: an answer ladder with a floor that needs no model. If the limiter predicts no room within 15 s, the request steps down instead of queueing: another model, then a lean answer, then cited passages with no model call, flagged as unchecked. The floor costs no model budget, so the system can still return a fallback even when the daily allowance is exhausted: it answered in 7 ms at the median (stand-in).

Where the time goes

real_burst-20261111T073012Z-0009 (Q14) took 15963 ms and the person left at 15.9 s, before any token. explain_request.py splits its spans into waiting 15122 ms, working 838 ms, retrying 0 ms and local work 3 ms. It waited 5625 ms for gpt-oss-120b, drafted in 838 ms, failed the citation check, then waited 9496 ms for a second draft on gpt-oss-20b. A retry in all but name, and the person got nothing.

When a limit is hit

Per minute (stand-in overload): 87.2% got a written answer (61.5% a weaker one, from a fallback model or the lean step), 7.7% got passages and 5.1% gave up waiting; the system rejected nobody outright. Anyone meeting an empty bucket waits at most until the ladder's limit (p95 queue wait 10572 ms). Per day: 0.0% written, 100.0% passages, because the ladder goes straight to the floor instead of holding people until a reset.

How this library will mislead a naive retriever

Plain BM25 on the raw question (data/retrieval/traps.md):

  • Different words. "How does H-Net decide where to cut the byte stream into pieces?" shares 0 of 7 terms with its answer, so it is never found; 0 of 5 settings got it right.
  • Text about a table beats the table. For "What training cost in FLOPs does Table 2 report for the big Transformer?" the table itself is rank 2.
  • Unanswerable but plausible. "What is H-Net's p95 latency serving 1,000 simultaneous requests?" pulls up the other paper (moshi) on the word "latency". The decide step refused it in 5 of 5 settings.

Retries

What ships: a failed call (429, server error or timeout) moves to the next model in the fallback order, each tried once, then down the ladder. Nothing is resent to the same model. Tested and rejected: 1 retry per step within 10% of recent calls left 13 and 14 people without a written answer, against 12 and 10 (stand-in, two seeds). Worse: a naive client (3 retries on the same model, no limiter) left 25 and 23 of 47 without one, against 0 and 0, and drew 274 and 264 rejections (stand-in).

What the person sees

At 3pm, with the daily budget gone, "What theoretical and practical latency does the paper report?" gets this after 8 ms (stand-in):

No written answer right now. Today's model allowance is used up, so please try again later. These are the closest passages from the papers, quoted as they are; they have not been checked against your question.

[S1] 2410.00037v1 pp.25-26 §5.3: "Table 5 reports results for ablations on the use of an RQ- Transformer. We first replicate the setting of Copet et al. (2023) with the delay pattern of [0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7], which means that each level of R VQ token is generated one timestep after the preceding level. In this context, we see that using an RQ-Transformer is not necessary, as it only provides a marginal improvement in perplexity. However, this delay pattern induces a theoretical latency of 8 timesteps, which amounts to 640ms, a latency that is incompatible with the requirements of a real-time dialogue model."

[S2] 2410.00037v1 p.26 §5.3: "Results - Additional ablations. Table 6 reports additional ablations on additional delay patterns, the weight of the semantic token loss and our proposed Inner Monologue procedure, all using the RQ-Transformer. First, we compare three configurations of delays that are compatible with real-time dialogue. The [0 , 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0] pattern represents the minimal latency of 80ms that can be obtained with Mimi tokens at 12.5Hz."

It is immediate, gives no time estimate it cannot back, and flags the passages as unchecked; here they miss the figures asked for (graded wrong), something a simple word-overlap check cannot detect.

What I'm unsure about, what I left out, what I learned

Least certain: Groq's daily token limit. Documented: 200000 a day. Observed: my key was charged 263495 gpt-oss-120b tokens in one rolling 24-hour window with no daily-limit rejection, so it seems to refill or reset rather than sum strictly, but I never saw the limit itself (confidence low). If it is lower than the limiter assumes, a call gets a 429 and falls to the next rung. Not built: sentence-by-sentence release (I buffer, so first token equals total) and a model-based grader (it would spend shared budget). Next: a router prompt for Q14, and fetching the neighbouring piece when a draft's number is missing (Q2).

One new thing, in my own words: One thing I learned is that a response starting quickly does not mean the system is fast. Time to first token is how long the person waits before seeing anything; total time is how long until the whole answer has arrived. At the model the two differ: the first piece arrives quickly and the rest streams after it. In my pipeline they are the same, because every reply is checked before it is released, so the person sees nothing until the checked answer is ready. That moved the question from "how fast does the model start" to "where does the waiting happen". Splitting one slow request into waiting, working and retrying showed it spent almost all its time waiting for budget, not generating. An average would have hidden that; percentiles and a per-request split did not.

Your feedback on Vaani

What I liked about Vaani was that it brings different parts of building a voice agent together in one platform. Instead of focusing only on speech recognition or voice generation, it also covers agent setup, tool integrations and call analysis. I think this makes it useful for developers looking to build practical voice-based applications.

One thing I found a little confusing was the latency information across the website. Different pages mention different response times, but it was not very clear what exactly was being measured or how the numbers compare.

One change I would suggest is adding a simple end-to-end example showing how someone can create an agent, connect it to tools, make a call and analyse the result. This would make the platform easier to understand, especially for someone exploring it for the first time.

How I worked with the assistant

My prompts from the main working session are attached verbatim (prompts.md).

Three prompts that mattered. First, the ground rules I set before any code: measure before designing, never make a person wait for budget that isn't coming, and every reported number must come from a file a script produced. They became the ledger with its caps, the answer ladder with a no-model floor, and the number checker behind this page. Second, "before any load testing, measure answer quality on real groq": quality became the first measurement, so the retrieval setting and the drafter were chosen on graded answers, not on speed. Third, the gate fix: "dont accept uncited sentences", and replay every saved real draft through the old and new gate before any real call. That turned a change I could only have shown with one anecdotal rerun into a measurement over every draft we had.

What it got wrong, and how I caught it. While grading, the assistant said a retrieved passage did not contain "discarding the prediction of the model for them" and treated an answer as unsupported. I rebuilt the pieces, found the phrase in the source, and asked it to recheck how it had decided; the answer stayed correct. I caught it by reading the source instead of the assistant's summary of it.

Where I overruled it. It proposed letting the citation gate accept an uncited opening or closing sentence. I refused: no sentence reaches a person without a citation; filler can be dropped, anything else fails the draft. Earlier it suggested counting the daily budget as a refilling bucket; I kept the conservative rolling sum until there was evidence, and when I later used refill accounting to avoid waiting, the honesty notes say so.

What is distinctly mine. The ground rules; the questions and their gold facts; the grading decisions and overrides; going back to one API key after re-reading the brief; and the call to stop spending budget once the replay had measured the fix.
